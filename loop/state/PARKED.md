- B-835 reviewer notes (not R2-valid, no real client affected): the 16 KiB cap is 5 bytes short of one full-size record, and crypto/tls reads a whole record before it parses the hello, so a legal hello padded to the maximum single record (16384 payload, 16389 wire) is refused as no_sni; real hellos with X25519MLKEM768 are 1.5 to 2 KiB and RFC 7685 padding targets 512 bytes, so this is hardening headroom only, closed by 16<<10 + 5 if ever wanted. A hello fragmented into 1-byte records still peeks under the cap. Per-slot peek memory is now about 50 to 65 KiB (buffer, crypto/tls rawInput, hand buffer), about 50 to 65 MiB at 1024 slots versus about 320 MiB before. The row's bytes.Repeat(record, 4) could be 2 and still fail the old cap; left as is to match the largest-hello wording.
- B-836 reviewer finding (not R2-valid, no in-scope attacker; for the maintainer before H-703 and S-1016 run the VM suite on the Mac): the product's host-network reader lists loopback aliases too, and escape/sandbox/workcell_darwin.go isolate wants CanaryAddr 192.175.48.129 as an lo0 alias, so on a Mac set up that way every Isolated row whose canary the product must reach (I5, I6, I11, B6 smoke) gets blocked_address; Linux avoids it with a local route (escape/suite/addr_linux.go localRoute).
